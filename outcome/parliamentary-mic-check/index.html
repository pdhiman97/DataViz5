ACT 0 — The Hook

Every session, thousands of questions.
Is it signal or noise?

Over five years, 559 MPs of the 17th Lok Sabha collectively filed over a lakh parliamentary questions across 63 ministries. That's an ocean of political attention — but where did it flow?

We mapped every question to eight thematic categories and shrank the entire parliamentary record down to a single number: 100 units of attention.

ACT 1 — The Breakdown

Shrink five years down to 100 units.
Here's who got the mic.

Health, education, and welfare dominate — nearly 22 units — roughly equal to State and special interest motions. Infrastructure and energy claim 18 units. Agriculture, despite being India's largest employer, gets 13.

Defence and foreign affairs, often the loudest in news cycles, represent just 3 units of actual parliamentary attention. The real work happens in the unglamorous middle.

ACT 2 — Party Playbooks

Does your party decide
what you talk about?

The ruling BJP coalition, with the most MPs and floor time, dominates every category by raw count. But the shape of attention differs strikingly.

BJP and allies concentrate more heavily in Finance and Infrastructure — the hallmarks of central scheme stewardship. The opposition — INC, DMK, AITC — lean into Health, Education, and Rights. Regional parties spike on State and Special Motions, the arena where local crises get parliamentary air.

ACT 3 — The Participation Gap

A handful of voices.
A silenced majority.

The Lok Sabha floor is not a democracy of voices. Out of 559 MPs tracked, the top 10% — just 55 people — made 42% of all debate appearances in the entire five-year term.

At the other extreme: the maximum any MP participated was 1,261 debates (Pushpendra Singh Chandel, BJP, UP). Several MPs recorded near-zero floor time despite high attendance. They show up. They just don't speak.

The average MP: 41 debates, 182 questions in five years. That's less than one question per working week.

ACT 4 — MP Archetypes

559 MPs.
4 parliamentary personalities.

Not all MPs engage with Parliament the same way. We classified all 559 tracked MPs into four archetypes based on debates participated, questions filed, and topic diversity.

The majority — 440 MPs (79%) — are Silent Observers: high attendance, but few floor appearances. Just 65 are Policy Hawks who combine high debate counts with cross-sector national policy work. 52 are Question Machines who work the system through written submissions.

ACT 5 — The Verdict

The mic was open.
But who spoke into it?

The 17th Lok Sabha's actual debate record tells a different story from the one on your news feed. The real parliamentary work — unglamorous, committee-level, question-hour-driven — happens in health, education, agriculture, and infrastructure, not in crisis motions.

But the floor itself is deeply unequal. A small cohort of highly active MPs carries the load while the majority attend, vote, and stay quiet.

The Parliamentary Mic Check isn't a judgment. It's a map. Now you know what the mic actually captured — and who was close to it.
