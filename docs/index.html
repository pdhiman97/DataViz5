Five Years of Roll Calls

What India’s 543 MPs actually did once the cameras turned off

By The Creative Technologist Team · 559 Member Profiles Analyzed · 101,999 Questions Tracked

When we watch Parliament on TV or viral social media clips, it looks like pure high school drama: walkouts, shouting matches, flying paper planes, and dramatic gestures in the well. Naturally, the public judges MPs like school kids: Did they show up? Media outlets celebrate 90% attendance like gold stars.

But parliamentary democracy is not won by sitting on leather benches with an ID badge. What happens after members swipe in for morning roll call? Who actually questions the executive, and who turns their microphone off for five full years?

17th Lok Sabha Swarm Nodes: 559 MPs
Step 01

The Diligent Classroom

Look at the attendance sheet for the 17th Lok Sabha (2019–2024). The national average attendance is 78.9%.

Almost every member crowds tightly between 75% and 95%. On paper, India’s parliament looks like a disciplined classroom filled with model students.

💡 Observation: Swarming along attendance alone creates a comforting illusion of legislative diligence.
Step 02

The Myth of the Register

Showing up is simple. What did they actually do once seated? Real oversight happens on paper: MPs submit formal questions that legally compel ministries to open files.

When you plot attendance against questions asked, the statistical correlation collapses to r = 0.14. Swiping your card in the morning tells you almost nothing about who is actually doing the work.

📊 Median Reference: National median is ~163 questions. Notice how widely MPs scatter vertically regardless of 90%+ attendance.
Step 03

The Cabinet Filter

Notice the cluster of dots resting flat on the floor line (0 questions). Did all of them skip work?

Not quite. 49 of those dots are Union Ministers (Amit Shah, Rajnath Singh, Kiren Rijiju, Nitin Gadkari). In parliamentary democracy, ministers answer questions; constitutionally, they are barred from asking them.

🏛️ Constitutional Rule: Ministers appear as hollow rings. The remaining 510 backbenchers carry the duty of questioning.
Step 04

Celebrities vs. Quiet Workhorses

With the Cabinet filtered, the real contrast emerges. Celebrity MPs often vanish once the cameras shut: Sunny Deol clocked 17% attendance and asked 4 questions in five full years; Shatrughan Sinha asked zero.

Meanwhile, obscure backbenchers filed over 600 questions each, carrying the day-to-day legislative oversight of the Republic.

⭐ Top Workhorses: Sukanta Majumdar (654 Qs), Supriya Sule (629 Qs & 16 Bills), Kuldeep Rai Sharma (610 Qs & 834 debates).
Step 05

The Maharashtra Anomaly

Geography fundamentally shapes scrutiny. Across party lines (BJP, NCP, Shiv Sena, INC), MPs from Maharashtra average 346.5 questions each—far higher than any other major state.

Six of the top ten question-askers in India come from Maharashtra alone. Compare that to Uttar Pradesh (averaging 125), Punjab (84), or Himachal Pradesh (27).

Step 06

The Gender Lens

Women MPs hold 82 out of 559 seats. While their average question volume closely mirrors men (177 vs 183), their thematic scrutiny shifts significantly.

Female MPs ask +24.2% more questions on Women & Child Development and +12.3% on Social Justice, while male MPs ask higher proportions on Civil Aviation (-22%) and Defence.

Step 07

The National Wishlist

When MPs write to Delhi, what are they asking for? We talk endlessly about space rockets and geopolitical statecraft, but Parliament runs on daily necessities.

Health (7,595), Agriculture (5,122), and Railways (4,809) soaked up over 17,500 questions. Meanwhile, high-tech ministries operated in near-total silence: Space received 1 question in five full years; Atomic Energy received 8.

🪐 The Space & Atomic Silence: Out of 101,999 questions filed, only 9 scrutinized the Department of Space and Atomic Energy combined.
